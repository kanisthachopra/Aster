# Music and Sound Brief

Developed on 2026-10-07 from the learner's spoken scene descriptions and soundtrack references. Companion to `prd.md > Music and Sound Direction`; the PRD remains canonical for product behavior. No audio has been composed, acquired, or auditioned in this research pass. Catalogs establish reference identities; creative descriptions below are proposed interpretations, not measured analyses of recordings. Exact excerpts are not yet identified for broad album references.

Implementation update later on 2026-10-07: exploration build 0.2 now includes an original temporary synthesized motif, layered chords, sparse reading phrases, reverberation, breathing and surface-dependent footsteps. Arrival has an airlock/goal cue, sequential power tones, rising motor whirr, ORBIT activation tones and six locally synthesized voice auditions. A shared arrival state drives subtitle changes and audio; dialogue ducks the music, with separate voice/effects/music controls. No named film soundtrack has been bundled or reproduced. These additions test the brief's timing and contrasts; the final orchestral score, expressive voice performances and human listening review remain unfinished production work.

Approved as the working musical direction on 2026-10-07 when the learner asked to continue forward after review. Approval concerns the written scene interpretation and production direction; audible prototypes will refine it during technical planning and building.

## The Intended Experience

The working interpretation is a solitary human discovering a vast world, finding a place to practice, and meeting a machine that becomes a companion. Awe gives way to curiosity, relief, and purposeful effort. Grand music has a place in arrival and discovery; pauses and small sounds give dialogue, reading, and decisions room. The learner identifies The Martian as the closest overall reference, so its human perspective anchors the proposal.

In a [first-person interview about The Martian](https://songexploder.net/transcripts/the-martian-transcript.pdf), Harry Gregson-Williams discusses optimism, scientific curiosity, humor, organic instruments alongside synth textures, and placing melody in gaps in dialogue. Those documented ideas support a score that feels both human and technological. They do not establish which excerpt the learner has in mind.

## One Score Across the Journey

Propose an original short theme, with a related small phrase for the robot. Reuse that identity in different textures so the app feels like one world. The references guide emotional qualities and scene construction; the production brief is for original music or appropriately licensed assets. No named soundtrack file is approved for embedding.

Proposed musical palette: warm strings, occasional piano or plucked/chiming notes, airy sustained electronic tones, and restrained rhythmic pulses. A broader ensemble can enter for the opening discovery. These are creative choices proposed from the combined references; the learner has not selected exact instruments, tempo, key, or composition. Keep the opening's grandeur and the menus' sparse texture as variations of the same identity.

## Scene Sequence

0. **Game title screen:** a representative still or subtly animated image behind a minimal left-side menu. This new entry screen is silent. Clicking Start activates audio and begins the body/suit inspection sequence below; it does not start with the theme already at full intensity.

1. **Inspect arms and suit:** near-silence, breathing, minute suit/body sounds. Establish a vulnerable person before bringing in the score.
2. **Recognize the world:** a sparse original theme expands in register and texture. Reach the opening peak as the cutscene ends; retain its sense of scale during the new/returning-user choice.
3. **Wait for the entry choice:** preserve the peak's emotional character with a sustainable held passage. This is a proposed way to honor "the crescendo stays" without repeating a loud build indefinitely. After selection, thin it into the mellow theme variation.
4. **Walk and explore:** the Narnia-assigned reference informs anticipation and movement; The Martian remains the overall anchor. Let the character's breathing and movement remain audible.
5. **Spot the park:** a short lyrical moment, informed by the learner's Casper placement, marks recognizing the destination. Do not play a full second theme over the entire route.
6. **Arrive at the park:** the Iwo Jima-assigned reference informs the arrival bed. Add a brief, distinct milestone cue when arrival is achieved, honoring the earlier God of War goal-cue reference. These are different musical functions rather than conflicting placements.
7. **Robot awakens:** electrical whirring and startup noises lead the physical activation. Soft chiming/plucked phrases enter as its individual presence emerges, following the American Beauty assignment. Transition into the positive, mellow robot-area theme.
8. **Station mini-cutscene:** avatar speech over quiet theme music. A slight pulse may underline effort, then recede when the holographic instructions appear.
9. **Instructions, upload, and controls:** silence or very low individual notes and three-to-four-note phrases, separated by substantial rests. Do not automatically fill every pause. Preserve the explicitly described opening choice as its own louder exception.
10. **Analysis:** quiet clicks and digital processing sounds support actual processing status. They are a decorative sound layer, not an audible claim that particular computations or pose matches succeeded. Stop the processing layer on completion, failure, or cancellation.
11. **Feedback:** robot explanations in short segments with matching visible text. Keep music quiet and lower it around speech. Preserve evidence and uncertainty in both channels.
12. **Successful station exit:** the milestone cue accompanies the actual completion notification and directs attention to the mission tab, over the low park background. Receiving analysis alone does not trigger this reward cue.
13. **Missions, journal, and Master Control:** return to sparse, reading-focused audio. Occasional robot lines can accompany real interactions; avoid unsolicited chatter repeatedly interrupting reading.

## Sound Behavior to Verify

- The music changes with scenes and interaction states, not only elapsed playback time; a slow reader does not push the score into the next scene.
- The first opening peak, park arrival cue, and completed-station reward remain distinct events.
- Scene transitions retain continuity without abrupt overlapping themes; the proposed shared phrase remains recognizable across sparse and grand versions.
- Spoken feedback is intelligible, corresponds to the displayed finding, and does not compete with processing sounds. Written feedback remains available with audio muted.
- Instructions and menu screens contain real pauses and low intensity. A review is not treated as a continuous action scene.
- Sound settings provide separate control over music, dialogue, and effects as a proposed addition to Master Control. Audio choices do not affect completion eligibility.
- Robot startup and breathing are treated as sounds of the character/world; music and interface cues are scored layers. The learner's term "non-diegetic" is retained as intent for voice presentation without forcing every sound into a single category.
